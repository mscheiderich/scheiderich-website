About Crissy

A Decade of Seeing Policies Actually Used

Crissy came into insurance through the side of the business most clients never see. Before managing the office, she spent years in claims support and policy administration — the work that happens after a loss is reported, when the wording of a policy stops being theoretical and starts determining what gets paid.

That history shapes how she writes coverage now. When she walks through a homeowners policy, she is thinking about the claim conversation that might follow in three years: whether the dwelling limit will still make sense, whether a deductible that looked attractive at signing will feel that way after a storm, whether a rider someone skipped is the one they will wish they had.

As office manager she also keeps the agency running day to day, which means she tends to be the person who knows where a file stands. Clients who prefer talking to someone who already has their history in front of them usually end up working with her.

Common Questions

Questions Clients Ask Crissy

Will filing a claim raise my rates?
It can, but not always, and the answer depends on the type of claim, who was at fault, and your history before it. Crissy can walk through how a specific claim is likely to be treated at renewal so you are not guessing. What she will not do is promise a particular outcome — carriers make that call, not the agency.
What does the office manager role mean for me as a client?
Practically, it means she sees the files moving through the office. If a document is outstanding or a renewal is coming up, she is usually the one who notices. Clients who dislike re-explaining their situation to a new person each time tend to prefer that.
Can she look at a policy I bought somewhere else?
Yes. Bring the declarations page — the summary sheet listing your limits and deductibles — and she will go through it with you. There is no charge for the review and no requirement to move the policy afterward. Sometimes the outcome is that your current coverage is fine.
I have had several policies and carriers over the years. Is that a problem?
Not at all, and it is a common reason people call her. Gaps in coverage history, lapsed policies, or claims spread across different carriers all affect how a new quote comes back. Sorting out that history first usually produces a more accurate quote than starting from a blank form.
