About Jennifer

The One Who Reads the Whole Page

A background in customer service and psychology turns out to be unusually good preparation for insurance. Both are mostly about noticing what a person has not said yet — the question they are embarrassed to ask, the part of the explanation where they quietly stopped following, the number that worried them but did not seem worth raising.

Jennifer works slowly on purpose. Coverage decisions get made once and then live in a drawer for years, so the twenty extra minutes spent making sure someone genuinely understands what they bought is time well spent. Clients who have felt rushed elsewhere tend to notice the difference immediately.

She is also the detail person. Declarations pages are long, repetitive, and easy to skim, which is exactly why something important occasionally hides in them. Jennifer reads all of it, and a fair number of her conversations start with a sentence like "did you know your policy does this?"

How It Works

What a Conversation With Jennifer Looks Like

Unhurried, and you can stop to ask something at any point.

STEP 01

You talk, she listens

The first part is mostly questions about your situation — what you own, what changed recently, what worried you enough to call. Nothing gets recommended before she understands the picture.

STEP 02

The explanation, at your pace

Each coverage is described by what it would do in a real situation, then checked against whether that situation applies to you. If a term does not land, she will find another way to say it rather than moving on.

STEP 03

You decide, with time

Options come in writing so you can reread them or talk them over at home. Coverage is in force only once a policy has been issued and confirmed — never on the basis of a phone call.

Common Questions

Questions People Ask Jennifer

I have never bought insurance before. Where do I even start?
With a conversation, not a form. Jennifer will ask what you are buying or protecting, who lives there, and what your lender requires if there is one. The vocabulary gets introduced as it becomes relevant rather than all at once. Plenty of her clients start by saying they do not know what they do not know, which is an honest and useful place to begin.
I have three quotes that all look the same. What am I supposed to compare?
The parts that are not on the front page — deductibles, dwelling and liability limits, and what each policy excludes. Two quotes can sit within a few dollars of each other monthly and behave very differently after a loss. Jennifer will read them alongside each other and point out where they genuinely diverge, including when the honest answer is that they are close to equivalent.
My rate went up and I want to switch. Is that the right move?
Sometimes, and sometimes the increase reflects something that would follow you to a new carrier anyway — a claim, a change in the property, or a broad rate adjustment. Jennifer looks at why it moved before recommending anything. Switching to a cheaper policy that covers less is not a saving, and she would rather explain that than quietly write it.
Can I ask questions that feel obvious?
Yes, and they usually are not obvious. Insurance uses ordinary words in specific ways, so "what does replacement cost actually mean" is a genuinely good question with a genuinely useful answer. Jennifer would rather spend the extra time up front than have you discover the answer during a claim.
