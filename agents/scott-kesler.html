About Scott

Details First, Recommendations Second

Scott's approach is easier to describe by what it is not. He does not lead with a product, he does not quote before understanding the situation, and he does not describe coverage in terms vague enough to sound reassuring while committing to nothing.

What he does instead is spend the first part of a conversation on specifics — the property, the vehicles, who drives them, what the mortgage requires, what the deadline is. Getting those details straight up front is what makes a quote accurate, and an accurate quote is what stops a policy from needing rework three weeks later.

That matters most when there is a clock involved. Closings, renewal dates, and mid-term coverage changes all have consequences for being late or being wrong. Scott works those to the date, and if something in your paperwork will not hold up, he would rather flag it early than discover it at the wrong moment.

Common Questions

Questions Clients Ask Scott

I am closing in two weeks. Is that enough time?
Usually, yes, and it helps to start now rather than at the end. What Scott needs is the property address, the closing date, your lender's details, and the loan number. With those in hand a binder can normally be issued and sent to the lender quickly. Timelines do depend on the property and how complete the information is, so earlier is always better than later.
I have three quotes and they all look similar. What am I missing?
Usually the deductibles, the dwelling or liability limits, and the exclusions — none of which show up in a headline premium. Two quotes can differ by a few dollars a month and by tens of thousands in what they would pay after a loss. Scott will read them against each other and show you where the actual differences sit.
Will you tell me if I do not need a coverage?
Yes. Recommending something that does not fit your situation creates a problem at renewal when you notice you are paying for it. If your current limits are reasonable or a rider does not apply to you, that is a more useful answer than an upsell.
What information should I have ready before calling?
For a home: the address, the year built, roughly the square footage, the roof age if you know it, and any claims in the last five years. For auto: drivers, vehicles, and your current limits. For a closing, add the lender and loan number. Nothing is required to start a conversation, but having those handy shortens it considerably.
